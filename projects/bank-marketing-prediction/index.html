Bank Marketing Prediction.

01 / THE BUSINESS QUESTION

Turning customer data into better outreach decisions.

Banks conduct marketing campaigns to encourage customers to subscribe to term-deposit products. Contacting every customer requires time and resources, while many customers will not subscribe.

This project investigates whether customer characteristics and historical campaign interactions can help identify potential subscribers and prioritize marketing outreach.

The goal is not to automate marketing decisions, but to evaluate whether predictive modeling can provide useful information for allocating outreach resources.

03 / EXPLORATORY DATA ANALYSIS

What the data revealed.

Exploratory analysis examined customer characteristics, previous campaign activity, contact methods, and subscription rates.

01

Previous contact matters

Customers with previous campaign contact had a higher subscription rate than customers with no previous contact.

23.07% Previously contacted
9.16% Never previously contacted

An observed association, not evidence that previous contact caused a subscription.

02

Previous campaign outcomes

Subscription rates varied considerably by the outcome of the previous campaign.

64.73% Subscription rate following a previous successful campaign

Historical campaign success was strongly associated with subsequent subscription behavior.

03

Contact timing varies

Subscription rates differed across months. March, December, September, and October had some of the highest observed rates.

51.99% March subscription rate

Monthly differences may reflect historical targeting practices and sample sizes, not necessarily seasonality.

04

Class imbalance

Only 11.7% of customers subscribed. A model that predicts every customer as a non-subscriber would still achieve 88.3% accuracy.

88.3% Majority-class baseline accuracy

This makes precision, recall, F1, and ranking metrics important for model evaluation.

04 / MODEL DEVELOPMENT

A leakage-aware modeling workflow.

The modeling process emphasized appropriate prediction timing, reproducible preprocessing, and evaluation beyond accuracy.

01

Prepare the data

Separate predictors and target, examine feature types, and create stratified training, validation, and test sets.

02

Prevent target leakage

Exclude duration, because call duration is only known after the current call. Represent prior campaign contact history with engineered features.

03

Build preprocessing pipelines

Apply standardization to numerical features for Logistic Regression and one-hot encoding to categorical features. Use scikit-learn pipelines to keep preprocessing within model training.

04

Compare classification models

Evaluate a majority-class baseline, Logistic Regression, and Random Forest using metrics appropriate for an imbalanced target.

05

Tune the decision threshold

Compare precision and recall at multiple thresholds using validation data, then evaluate the selected thresholds on the held-out test set.

Prediction-time consideration

The model is designed for pre-call prioritization. The campaign feature was transformed into contacts_before_current_call by subtracting one, under the assumption that the source count includes the current call.

08 / BUSINESS IMPLICATIONS

From model output to actionable questions.

Recommendations

  • Use predicted probabilities to help prioritize customers for marketing outreach.
  • Investigate differences in subscription rates across contact months and communication channels.
  • Consider previous campaign outcomes when evaluating follow-up opportunities.
  • Select classification thresholds based on outreach capacity, acquisition costs, and expected subscription value.

Limitations

  • Historical campaign data may not reflect current customer behavior or marketing effectiveness.
  • Predictive associations do not establish causation.
  • The selected F1 threshold does not necessarily maximize financial return.
  • The model should be validated on newer campaigns and reviewed for fairness before operational use.
